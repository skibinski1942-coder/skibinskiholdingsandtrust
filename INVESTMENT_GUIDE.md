## 1. Understanding the Stock Market

The stock market is a marketplace where shares of publicly listed
companies are bought and sold. Key participants include retail investors,
institutional investors, market makers, and brokers.

**Key indices to monitor:**
- **S&P 500** — 500 large U.S. companies; broad market benchmark.
- **NASDAQ Composite** — technology-heavy index.
- **Dow Jones Industrial Average (DJIA)** — 30 blue-chip U.S. companies.

**Market hours (NYSE / NASDAQ):**
- Regular session: 9:30 AM – 4:00 PM ET
- Pre-market: 4:00 AM – 9:30 AM ET
- After-hours: 4:00 PM – 8:00 PM ET

## 2. Day Trading

Day trading is the practice of buying and selling securities **within the
same trading day**, closing all positions before the market closes.

### Core Principles
- **Capital preservation first** — protect your principal above all else.
- **Risk/Reward ratio** — target at least a 2:1 reward-to-risk on every
  trade (e.g., risk $1 to make $2).
- **Position sizing** — never risk more than 1–2% of total capital on a
  single trade.
- **Cut losses quickly** — use hard stop-loss orders on every position.

### Pattern Day Trader (PDT) Rule
In the U.S., if you execute **4 or more day trades in 5 rolling business
days** in a margin account, FINRA classifies you as a Pattern Day Trader.
PDT accounts must maintain a **minimum equity of $25,000**.

### Common Day-Trading Strategies
| Strategy | Description |
|---|---|
| Momentum | Buy stocks moving strongly in one direction on high volume |
| Scalping | Very short holding periods (seconds to minutes); small per-trade gains |
| Breakout | Enter when price breaks above resistance or below support |
| Reversal | Trade against a trend when signs of exhaustion appear |

### Essential Tools
- Level 2 quotes (order-book depth)
- Real-time charting with technical indicators (VWAP, RSI, MACD, Bollinger Bands)
- Stock screeners (unusual volume, gap-up/gap-down, relative strength)

## 3. Margin Trading

A **margin account** lets you borrow money from your broker to purchase
more securities than your cash balance would otherwise allow.

### How It Works
- **Initial margin** — minimum equity required to open a leveraged
  position (Reg T: 50% in the U.S.).
- **Maintenance margin** — minimum equity required to keep a position
  open (typically 25–30%).
- **Margin call** — if account equity falls below maintenance margin, the
  broker demands additional funds or liquidates positions.

### Risks
- Losses are amplified proportionally to leverage used.
- Interest accrues daily on borrowed funds.
- Forced liquidation can happen at the worst possible price.

**Rule of thumb:** Use margin only when you have high-conviction trades
and a clear, pre-defined exit strategy.

## 4. Short Selling

Short selling is the sale of a security **you do not currently own**,
with the intention of buying it back later at a lower price to profit
from the price decline.

### Mechanics
1. Borrow shares from a broker.
2. Sell them at the current market price.
3. Wait for the price to fall.
4. Buy the shares back (cover) at the lower price.
5. Return shares to the broker and pocket the difference (minus fees).

### Key Risks
- **Unlimited loss potential** — a stock can theoretically rise
  indefinitely, so losses are not capped.
- **Short squeeze** — heavily shorted stocks can spike violently when
  short sellers rush to cover at the same time.
- **Borrow fees** — hard-to-borrow shares carry high daily borrow rates.

### Short Interest Metrics
- **Short interest ratio (days to cover)** — total shares short ÷ average
  daily volume. High values (>10) may signal a squeeze risk.
- **Short % of float** — short shares as a percentage of freely tradable
  shares.

## 5. Mergers & Acquisitions (M&A)

Mergers and acquisitions create opportunities through a strategy called
**merger arbitrage** (risk arbitrage).

### Key Deal Types
| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| All-cash acquisition | Acquirer pays a fixed price per share in cash |
| Stock-for-stock merger | Target shareholders receive acquirer shares at a ratio |
| Leveraged buyout (LBO) | Acquirer uses significant debt to finance the purchase |

### Merger Arbitrage Basics
- When an acquisition is announced, the target's stock usually jumps to
  near (but slightly below) the offer price.
- The **spread** (difference between offer price and current trading
  price) reflects deal risk and time value.
- Arbitrageurs buy the target (and sometimes short the acquirer in
  stock deals) to capture the spread.
- Profit is realized when the deal closes; a deal break causes a sharp
  loss.

## 6. Building Generational Wealth

Generational wealth is financial capital that is preserved and grown
across multiple generations. Day trading and active strategies can
**accelerate** capital accumulation, but the foundation of generational
wealth rests on long-term, diversified investing.

### Wealth-Building Framework

```
Short Term (0–2 years)   → Active trading, options, event-driven plays
Medium Term (2–10 years) → Growth equities, sector ETFs, real estate
Long Term (10+ years)    → Index funds, dividend reinvestment, private equity
Preservation             → Tax-advantaged accounts, estate planning, trusts
```

### Core Principles
1. **Compound interest** — reinvest gains to let your money grow
   exponentially over time. A 10% annual return doubles capital every
   ~7 years (Rule of 72).
2. **Diversification** — spread risk across asset classes (equities,
   fixed income, real estate, commodities, alternatives).
3. **Tax efficiency** — maximize contributions to tax-advantaged
   accounts (401k, IRA, Roth IRA). Harvest losses to offset gains.
4. **Estate planning** — use trusts, wills, and beneficiary designations
   to transfer wealth with minimal tax friction.
5. **Financial literacy** — continuously educate every generation on
   money management, investing, and entrepreneurship.

### Suggested Asset Allocation (Growth Phase)

|---|---|

| International equities | 20% |
| Real estate / REITs | 15% |
| Fixed income / bonds | 10% |
| Alternative investments | 10% |
| Cash / equivalents | 5% |

Adjust allocations based on time horizon, risk tolerance, and market
conditions.

## 7. Risk Management Rules

1. **Never risk more than 2% of total capital on a single trade.**
2. **Always set a stop-loss before entering a position.**
3. **Do not average down into losing positions without a clear thesis.**
4. **Keep a trading journal** — log every entry, exit, thesis, and
   outcome to identify patterns and improve discipline.
5. **Separate trading capital from long-term investment capital.**
6. **Avoid trading with money you cannot afford to lose.**
